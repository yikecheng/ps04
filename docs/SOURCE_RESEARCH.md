# Source strategy and missing perspectives

## Independence judgments

Publisher IDs represent the producer of the evidence, not the discovery endpoint. A company blog, its YouTube channel, and a podcast appearance do not become independent merely because they use different formats. A podcast episode and a YouTube copy of that recording would count as one underlying item.

The corpus deliberately mixes:

- vendor capability and workflow announcements from GitHub, Cursor, Sourcegraph, and OpenAI;
- independent or author-led research found through arXiv and retained research pages;
- practitioner and industry interviews from InfoQ;
- creator demonstrations from IBM Technology, Kevin Stratvert, Simon Scrapes, and GitHub's channel.

Vendor pages establish shipped scope and reported measurements. Research supplies inspectable task definitions and methods but can be narrow or pre-publication. Practitioner accounts expose operating problems but do not establish prevalence. Creator videos can show workflows while carrying attention, sponsorship, or course-selling incentives.

## Discovery fallbacks

GitHub's live changelog and InfoQ's AI/engineering feed are genuine RSS discovery routes. ArXiv category feeds are queried even when their current response contributes no matching item. Several official YouTube channel-feed endpoints returned errors in the first live attempt; individually verified watch pages are labeled as a page fallback rather than misrepresented as successful feed discovery. Sourcegraph, Cursor, and selected research pages are labeled supplemental publisher-index items.

## Exclusions and deferrals

Mechanical filtering excludes invalid, future, unknown, and out-of-window dates. Editorial filtering rejects generic beginner material, availability announcements without production outcomes, duplicated positioning, and claims irrelevant to small-team decisions. It defers plausible numerical claims when the primary source, applicable version, or method cannot be inspected.

## Missing perspectives

The evidence still lacks a controlled head-to-head study of common small-team coding tasks across the same repositories, supervision levels, acceptance tests, and maintenance horizon. It also underrepresents failed internal pilots, junior engineers, non-English teams, accessibility impacts, and downstream maintainers who absorb agent-created rework. Vendor adoption volume cannot fill these gaps.
