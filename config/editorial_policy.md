# Agent Signal Editorial Policy — Version 2

## Purpose and scope

Agent Signal helps technical leads at small software teams decide whether and how to adopt AI coding agents. We cover changes that affect production engineering decisions: delegation, context and harness design, verification, security, cost, team workflow, and maintenance. We exclude generic AI news, model-release scorekeeping without workflow implications, beginner prompting tutorials, investment speculation, and claims whose publication date or supporting passage cannot be verified.

## Selection criteria

Assess every eligible item at the level of the claims we may use, not by publisher prestige or model confidence.

1. **Audience relevance:** Prefer evidence that can change a small team's adoption, workflow, controls, or near-term watch list.
2. **Freshness:** Supporting material must fall within the mechanical 30-date window. A refreshed page or re-upload does not make an old event new. State what is newly reported when recent material discusses older events.
3. **Claim-level credibility:** Look for an inspectable method, direct observation, reproducible artifact, named example, or precise limitation. Institutional reputation and popularity are context, not proof.
4. **Novelty:** Prefer genuinely new capabilities, measurements, failure modes, or practitioner lessons. Repeated coverage of one announcement is one underlying claim.
5. **Perspective diversity:** Build a mix of vendor capability claims, independent research, and practitioner experience. Do not let one company or distribution channel define the story.

## Evidence and incentives

- A product announcement may establish availability and workflow scope. A production-readiness conclusion additionally requires an independent outcome measure or a clearly labeled local pilot.
- Treat vendor benchmarks as evidence of what a vendor reports, not independent proof of effectiveness.
- Record relevant incentives: a vendor sells the featured tool; an investor promotes a portfolio thesis; a creator benefits from attention; a researcher may be working with a bounded sample or benchmark.
- Corroboration requires evidence that is independent in origin. Syndication, reposts, reactions to the same press release, and a podcast's YouTube copy do not count separately.
- Prefer exact passages and timestamped transcript segments. Every numerical claim must retain its denominator, baseline, task definition, and measured outcome when available; name missing elements in the decision reason.
- Separate activity and efficiency metrics such as merged changes, token use, latency, or cache hits from accepted outcomes such as held-out pass rate, defects, rework, rollback, and maintainability.
- Defer creator-reported numerical improvements when the primary source, applicable product version, or method is not inspectable. A disclaimer does not make an unsupported number usable.
- Do not infer causation from usage or activity metrics.

## Uncertainty and disagreement

- Distinguish reported facts, attributed source claims, and our interpretation.
- When credible sources conflict, explain differences in task, sample, measurement, incentives, or operating conditions before deciding whether there is a real contradiction.
- Use calibrated language. Say “suggests,” “in this sample,” or “the vendor reports” when the evidence does not justify a general conclusion.
- Do not manufacture balance. A limitation or counterargument must be supported by evidence or by a clearly stated boundary of the available evidence.
- Defer an item when it may become useful but lacks a transcript, date, inspectable support, or enough context. Reject it when it is irrelevant, duplicative, promotional without usable evidence, or substantively unreliable.

## Series roles

- **Piece 1 — What changed:** Select consequential developments and explain why they matter now.
- **Piece 2 — What survives scrutiny:** Compare capability claims with research, verification needs, and practitioner experience.
- **Piece 3 — What to do next:** Derive bounded actions and watch signals for small teams without presenting speculation as fact.

Every accepted item must have a stated purpose in at least one piece. Each piece must use at least three independent publishers, contain a multi-source inference whose reasoning is explicit, and acknowledge a meaningful limitation.
